Who Is Jeremiah?

Dad. Engineer. Builder. Constant learner.

I’m Jeremiah O’Neal. Before I’m a candidate, I’m a dad and a member of this community. My career has been built around figuring out how complicated systems work, asking what can fail, listening to the people closest to the problem and finding practical ways to make things better.

That same curiosity is a big part of why I’m running for school board. I don’t believe leadership means walking into a room with every answer. I believe it means being willing to learn, ask better questions and do the work required to understand the answer.

How I think
“I like learning how things work — especially when understanding the system can help solve a real problem for someone.”

🔧 Practical problem solving

My background ranges from mechanical systems and power infrastructure to controls, networking, automation and embedded electronics. I tend to approach a challenge by breaking it into parts, understanding the failure points and testing solutions.

📚 Always learning

I’m constantly teaching myself something new—technology, engineering, software, public policy, accessibility tools or whatever the problem in front of me requires. I don’t see “I don’t know yet” as a stopping point.

👥 People before systems

Technical knowledge matters, but systems only work when the people using and operating them are heard. Some of the most important information comes from the teacher, technician, aide, parent or employee dealing with the issue every day.

📈 Measure what matters

In engineering, reliability has to be more than a slogan. You identify risks, track performance, investigate failures and improve. I believe public organizations benefit from the same discipline—clear goals, useful data and honest follow-through.

Personal R&D Project

ATLAS

Augmentative communication, approached from a builder’s perspective.

ATLAS grew from a very personal question: could a useful physical-first AAC system be made simpler, more customizable and more affordable?

Building to learn

A real problem became a technology project.

ATLAS is a modular augmentative and alternative communication concept I’ve been developing through hands-on prototyping. The project combines physical communication pages, embedded electronics, audio playback, sensors and software with an accessibility-first design philosophy.

Developing it has pushed me deeper into microcontrollers, embedded firmware, sensors, low-voltage electronics, audio systems, prototyping and human-centered design. It has also reinforced something I already believed: technology is most valuable when it removes a barrier for a real person.

The project is still evolving. That is part of what I enjoy about it—the process of researching, testing, failing, adjusting and learning enough to make the next version better.
