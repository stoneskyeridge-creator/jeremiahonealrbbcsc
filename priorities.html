Campaign priorities

Ask better questions. Build on what works.

I don't have all the answers, and a school-board candidate shouldn't pretend to. My approach is simple: understand what RBBCSC already does, listen to the people closest to the work, study proven ideas, measure results and improve where the evidence says we should.

Keep what works. Measure whether it works. Improve what can be improved. Fix what isn't working.

Special Education Innovation

Move beyond simply asking what services exist. Ask whether students have the right communication, access, sensory, mobility and independence supports—and whether those supports are producing meaningful outcomes.

What barriers are preventing a student from participating, communicating or becoming more independent—and can we remove them?

Teachers & Staff

Good oversight requires hearing from teachers, aides, therapists, transportation, custodial, maintenance, food service, office, technology and leadership staff.

Do you feel supported? Heard? Safe raising legitimate concerns? Do concerns receive meaningful follow-up? Why do good employees leave—and what would make them stay?

Career Pathways & Real-World Experience

Build on existing career and technical opportunities while exploring more ways for students to experience real work before graduation.

  • Skilled trades, construction, HVAC, electrical, mechanics and heavy equipment
  • Cosmetology and other licensed career fields
  • IT, web development, cybersecurity and district technology
  • Accounting, bookkeeping, Excel and office administration
  • Transportation, facilities, engineering, landscaping and operations

Could district projects, departments and contractor agreements create supervised job-shadowing, internships or apprenticeships where legally and practically appropriate?

STEAM, Coding & AI

RBBCSC already has STEAM and technology infrastructure. The question is how deeply students are using it to build practical skills.

  • Coding and computational thinking
  • Microcontrollers, electronics and robotics
  • CAD, 3D printing and fabrication
  • Responsible AI literacy and emerging technology
  • Real projects tied to community and accessibility needs

Are students primarily consuming technology—or learning how to build, troubleshoot and responsibly use it?

Transparency & Oversight

Public information should be understandable and usable—not merely technically available. Board members should ask hard questions about budgets, staffing, contracts, outcomes and whether decisions are delivering value.

Can a resident reasonably understand where dollars go, what the board decided and why?

Leadership Connected to Classrooms

Board and district leadership should understand day-to-day realities across grade levels, buildings and programs. That means more than reports and presentations.

Explore regular classroom visits, listening sessions, time with teachers and support staff, observing programs and seeing operational challenges firsthand—without micromanaging educators or interfering with instruction.

Are decision-makers close enough to the work to understand the impact of their decisions?

The standard

Research first. Promise second.

These are priorities and ideas worth investigating—not promises that every idea will be implemented. Before supporting a major change, I want to understand the current program, cost, legal and operational requirements, evidence, staff and family perspectives, and how success would be measured.
