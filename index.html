Why I’m Running
A parent first.

Special education helped bring me to the table. Serving on the board would mean representing every student, family, school and employee across RBBCSC.

My approach

Leadership should begin with questions, not assumptions.

My family’s experience with special education showed me how important it is for parents to ask questions, speak up, and keep looking for better solutions when something can be better for a child.

I want to hear from teachers, aides, therapists, bus drivers, custodians, cafeteria staff, principals, directors, parents, students and community members who see the day-to-day realities of our schools.

A board member should provide oversight, ask difficult but fair questions, understand the facts, insist on transparency, and make decisions based on what best serves students and the long-term strength of the district.

Campaign priorities

Practical ideas. Measurable outcomes. Students first.

Click any priority to see the questions I believe the board should be asking.

♿

Special Education

Better support, communication, inclusion, safety and evidence-based tools.

Are staffing and supports matched to student needs? Are families receiving timely, meaningful communication? Are we using technology and proven practices effectively? Are students included with general-education peers whenever appropriate?
🧑‍🏫

Teachers & Staff

Listen before good people leave. Understand workload, support and turnover.

What do exit patterns tell us? Are concerns reaching decision-makers early enough? Are employees supported with realistic workloads, training and resources?
🔧

Trades & Careers

Expand pathways into well-paying careers through partnerships and hands-on learning.

Can students access more electrical, HVAC, construction, auto, engineering, advanced manufacturing and computing opportunities? Which local employers and colleges can partner with RBBCSC?
🤖

STEAM, Coding & AI

Prepare students for the world they are entering without losing the fundamentals.

Where can age-appropriate coding, microcontrollers, electronics, robotics, engineering and AI literacy strengthen existing curriculum and career pathways?
🔎

Transparency & Oversight

Clear data, clear explanations and consistent follow-through on major decisions.

Can taxpayers easily understand major spending decisions? Are board questions and follow-up visible? Do we consistently compare costs, alternatives and outcomes?
🏫

Leadership in Schools

Keep central-office decisions connected to classroom reality.

Could district leaders spend structured time working directly in classrooms and schools? Could that strengthen decision-making while helping with substitute and staffing pressure?
