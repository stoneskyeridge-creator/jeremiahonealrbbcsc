Why this matters to me
A parent first.

My family’s experience with special education showed me how important it is for parents to ask questions, speak up, and keep looking for better solutions when something can be better for a child.

That experience brought me to the table — but the responsibility of a board member is to every student, every family, and every employee across RBBCSC.

My approach

Leadership should begin with questions, not assumptions.

I want to hear from the teachers, aides, therapists, bus drivers, custodians, cafeteria staff, principals, directors, parents, students, and community members who see the day-to-day realities of our schools.

A board member should provide oversight, ask difficult but fair questions, understand the facts, insist on transparency, and make decisions based on what best serves students and the long-term strength of the district.

Campaign priorities

Practical ideas. Measurable outcomes. Students first.

These priorities are starting points for thoughtful board-level oversight and community discussion — not promises that one person can impose alone.

♿

Special Education Innovation

Evaluate communication supports, classroom technology, sensory and regulation tools, inclusion opportunities, data tracking, safety practices, staffing, and proven approaches used successfully elsewhere.

🧑‍🏫

Teachers & Staff

Listen before good people leave. Understand staffing pressure, turnover, classroom support needs, workload, training, and whether leadership gets enough direct feedback from employees.

🔧

Trades & Career Pathways

Strengthen pathways into electrical, HVAC, auto body, engineering, construction, advanced manufacturing, computing, and other skilled careers through partnerships and hands-on opportunities.

🤖

STEAM, Coding & AI

Prepare students with age-appropriate coding, electronics, microcontrollers, engineering, robotics, AI literacy, and hands-on problem-solving while keeping strong fundamentals at the center.

🔎

Transparency & Oversight

Ask for clear data, understandable financial reporting, follow-up on major concerns, stronger communication, and public explanations that help taxpayers understand decisions.

🏫

Leadership in the Classroom

Explore ways for central-office leaders to spend meaningful time directly in classrooms and schools so decisions stay connected to day-to-day reality and staffing pressure.
