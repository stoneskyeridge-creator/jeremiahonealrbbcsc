A local discussion proposal

What Could Neurodiversity-Affirming Support Look Like at RBBCSC?

This proposal asks RBBCSC to begin by listening and studying—not by assuming the answer in advance. One possible first step is a Neurodiversity & Special Education Family Advisory Committee bringing families, educators, support staff, administrators and neurodivergent voices together.

I’M INTERESTED IN HELPING → READ THE BOULDER STORY ↗
Who could be at the table

Families, educators, staff and neurodivergent voices.

Possible members could include parents and guardians, general- and special-education teachers, paraeducators, SLP/OT/school psychology and related-service staff, administrators, student-support personnel, and neurodivergent students, graduates or adult community members when appropriate.

What the group could examine

Practical conditions that help students learn.

Student engagement, behavior and regulation, inclusion and LRE, sensory and environmental needs, AAC and communication, agency and autonomy, family knowledge, and the training, coaching and collaboration staff need.

What can we learn from our students, families and educators about the conditions that help neurodivergent students feel safe, connected, regulated, engaged and able to learn—and how can we create those conditions more consistently across RBBCSC?
A practical first step

Explore first. Build locally.

A small exploratory group could review Boulder Valley’s model and other relevant approaches, hold listening sessions, identify practices already working well in RBBCSC, and return to district leadership and the School Board with recommendations. This does not predetermine a policy outcome; it creates a structured way to develop recommendations appropriate for RBBCSC.
