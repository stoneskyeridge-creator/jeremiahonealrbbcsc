Special Education • Autism • Robotics
Yale University • Science Robotics • August 22, 2018

Can socially assistive robots help some children with autism practice social communication?

A Yale-led study examined a one-month, home-based intervention involving 12 children with ASD ages 6–12. Children interacted with an autonomous social robot and a caregiver for about 30 minutes a day for 30 days through activities involving emotional storytelling, perspective-taking and other social skills.

Yale reported improvements in measured social behaviors, while caregivers reported improvements including eye contact and initiating communication. Researchers also reported that improvements persisted when the robot was not present. The study was small, so this should be viewed as promising research to understand—not proof that a particular robot or program should automatically be adopted by a school district.

Why this matters for RBBCSCSpecial education innovation should include watching emerging tools that may help students engage, communicate and practice skills. Socially assistive robotics could be an area to study alongside AAC, eye gaze, switch access, sensory supports and other assistive technologies.

The question to ask: Is this an emerging technology worth studying, partnering with a university on, or eventually evaluating through a carefully designed pilot?
