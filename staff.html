Teachers & Staff

Listen before good people leave.

RBBCSC already has employee organizations and formal ways for staff to communicate. The harder question is whether concerns are consistently heard, investigated, followed up on and acted on when appropriate.

I don't want to assume I already know why people stay, why people leave or what every employee needs. I want the board asking the people doing the work.

Questions worth asking

What is it actually like to work here?

Do you feel supported in doing your job well?
Do you feel heard when you raise a legitimate concern?
When concerns are raised, do you receive meaningful follow-up?
Do you have the staffing, training, time and resources you need?
Is your workload reasonable and sustainable?
Do you feel comfortable disagreeing with leadership?
Have you worried that raising a concern could negatively affect your assignment, evaluation, opportunities or treatment?
Do you believe problems are investigated consistently and fairly?
Why do good employees leave RBBCSC?
What would make RBBCSC a better place to build a career?

Asking about retaliation is not the same as claiming retaliation is occurring. A healthy organization should be willing to ask whether employees feel psychologically and professionally safe raising concerns—and examine the answers.

Every employee matters

“Staff” means more than teachers.

Closest to students

Teachers, instructional assistants, special-education staff, therapists, counselors, nurses and other student-support professionals.

Keeping schools running

Custodians, maintenance, grounds, transportation, bus mechanics, food service, office staff and technology staff.

School & district leadership

Principals, directors, administrators and central-office staff also have perspectives the board should understand.

The goal

Identify patterns, understand staffing and resource pressures, learn from exit feedback and find practical retention solutions rather than guessing why turnover happens.

Board responsibility

Listening requires follow-through.

Providing a channel for feedback is only the beginning. Good oversight means listening, asking follow-up questions, investigating when appropriate, identifying recurring patterns, communicating outcomes when possible and taking action when the evidence supports it.

Listen → Ask Questions → Investigate → Follow Up → Act when appropriate.

Turnover & retention

Understand why good people leave.

Personnel records show employee turnover, but a resignation record alone does not tell us why someone left. I do not want to assign motives without evidence.

Look for trends

Review turnover by role, building and year where appropriate, while respecting employee privacy.

Learn from exits

Understand what exit feedback is collected, whether recurring themes are identified and whether those themes reach the board.

Listen to current staff

Retention isn't only about people who already left. Ask current employees what helps them succeed and what makes their work unnecessarily difficult.

Measure improvement

If changes are made, determine whether retention, vacancies, workload or employee feedback actually improves.
