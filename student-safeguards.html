Proposed action for RBBCSC consideration

Enhanced Safety, Care Compliance, Documentation & Video Monitoring

The substantive framework below comes from a formal request submitted to RBBCSC by families of students receiving special education services. Family names, student names, signatures and other identifying details are not published here. The proposal is presented so the broader community can read the safeguards themselves and decide whether they support formal consideration by the district.

This is a proposed framework—not an adopted RBBCSC policy. It is being shared for community review, discussion and support.

Purpose of the Request

Families of students receiving special education services within Richland-Bean Blossom Community School Corporation requested additional safeguards for students with significant communication, developmental, behavioral, medical, personal-care, mobility, or supervision needs.

Many students depend heavily upon adults throughout the school day to meet needs they cannot independently manage. At the same time, some students cannot reliably communicate that a required need was missed or explain what happened when something goes wrong.

For students with these limitations, simply having a written Individualized Education Program (IEP), care plan, or schedule does not by itself provide assurance that critical needs are consistently met.

PREVENT
Help prevent required supports and care from being missed.
VERIFY
Create objective documentation that required supports occurred, and provide an objective record when significant incidents happen.
INFORM
Ensure families receive reliable information about their child's care and supports each school day.

The proposal therefore requests: (1) a district-approved electronic care and support compliance system with active reminders, overdue alerts, escalation, documentation, and daily parent reporting; and (2) district-operated video monitoring in appropriate high-support special education classrooms.

Section 1: Electronic Care and Support Compliance System

A district-approved electronic system should be accessible to responsible staff through an iPad, computer, or other appropriate district device for students whose individualized needs warrant this level of support. The system should actively assist staff in ensuring required supports are provided when needed, rather than merely documenting events after the fact.

Individualized requirements. Tracked items should be determined by each student's IEP, health plan, behavioral plan, safety plan, or other applicable plan. Items may include toileting and personal care; feeding and hydration; medication and health activities; AAC access and communication opportunities; sensory and regulation supports; behavioral supports; positioning and mobility; transitions; accommodations; scheduled checks; prompting or adult assistance; functional-independence opportunities; and other required disability-related supports.

Contemporaneous verification. Responsible staff should document completion at or near the time a support occurs. The record should identify what was required, whether it was completed, approximate time when relevant, exceptions such as delay, refusal, or missed or partial completion, relevant outcome information, and the responsible staff member or role when appropriate.

Section 2: Active Alerts and Escalation

For identified time-sensitive or critical supports, the system should do more than record a missed item. When an item has not been documented within its required timeframe, the system should generate an alert to responsible personnel. If unresolved, notification should escalate according to an established protocol appropriate to the seriousness of the need, including designated backup staff, teacher, nurse, administrator, or other appropriate responsible person.

Legitimate exceptions—including student refusal, absence, change in medical direction, emergency circumstances, or appropriate rescheduling—should be documented without mischaracterizing them as staff failures.

The purpose is not merely to document compliance after the fact. It is to actively support compliance by identifying required supports that have not occurred, notifying responsible personnel while corrective action can still be taken, and escalating unresolved critical items according to an established protocol.

For students who cannot reliably identify or report an unmet need, safeguards should be designed to identify a missed critical need without relying upon the student to report it.

Section 3: Responsibility and Backup Coverage

For critical or time-sensitive needs, there should be a clearly established process identifying responsibility and how it transfers when the responsible person is unavailable. Staff absence, breaks, classroom coverage changes, substitute staffing, or reassignment should not result in a required student need being unintentionally overlooked. Temporary staff assuming responsibility should have appropriate access to essential information necessary to safely implement required supports.

Section 4: Daily Parent Care and Support Report

Parents or guardians of participating students should receive a concise daily electronic summary generated from the contemporaneous records created during the school day, rather than reconstructed solely from staff recollection at dismissal.

The report should identify, as applicable: required tracked supports; whether each was completed; approximate completion time when relevant; delayed, missed, refused, partial, or otherwise incomplete items; relevant explanations; escalation or corrective action taken; and other information identified by the student's Case Conference Committee (CCC) as appropriate for daily reporting.

The report should be provided automatically without requiring families to request it each day. It must not replace immediate parent notification when a serious event requires more timely communication, including significant injury, medical emergency, medication error, missed critical care, restraint or seclusion, elopement, significant safety event, or another circumstance requiring prompt notification.

Section 5: Supervisory Review and Pattern Identification

Appropriate school personnel should periodically review data for patterns of repeatedly missed, delayed, refused, or difficult-to-implement supports. Repeated problems should result in appropriate review rather than becoming accepted as routine. Data may also help a student's CCC determine whether supports are effective, procedures require adjustment, independence is increasing, or the student's IEP or other plans require modification.

Section 6: Protection for Students and Staff

These safeguards can protect school employees as well as students. Electronic records can demonstrate that required supports were appropriately provided, and video can substantiate appropriate staff actions when questions or allegations arise. Objective information can reduce misunderstandings, improve investigations, identify areas requiring improvement, and strengthen trust.

These safeguards do not replace qualified staff, appropriate staffing, supervision, professional judgment, IEP implementation, incident reporting, or communication with families. They are additional tools intended to help everyone responsible for vulnerable students succeed.

Section 7: Video Monitoring in Appropriate Special Education Classrooms

The proposal requests district-operated video monitoring in appropriate high-support special education classrooms, particularly those serving students whose disabilities substantially limit their ability to independently report or describe significant events. Some students cannot reliably explain:

  • How an injury occurred
  • What happened before a behavioral or safety incident
  • Whether another person was involved
  • What occurred during an emergency
  • Whether an interaction frightened or harmed them
  • What occurred during restraint or seclusion, or other circumstances reasonably requiring investigation

Video can provide an objective source of information in these situations.

Indiana lawmakers considered statewide legislation—House Bill 1073 (2024) and House Bill 1285 (2025)—that would have required recording equipment in special education classrooms, sensory rooms, and seclusion or time-out spaces. Neither bill was enacted. This is included as policy context only; this proposal does not assert a current statewide legal mandate and asks RBBCSC to consider a local safeguard.

Privacy. The proposal does not request cameras in bathrooms, changing areas, or other locations requiring personal privacy; unrestricted parental livestream access; or cameras for routine monitoring or evaluation of individual employees. It requests an objective record when a significant event occurs involving a vulnerable student who may be unable to independently explain what happened.

Section 8: Video Preservation Following Significant Incidents

Potentially relevant footage should be promptly preserved following a significant incident so routine system overwriting does not eliminate the record before appropriate review. Examples may include significant or unexplained injury; suspected abuse, neglect, or mistreatment; restraint or seclusion; significant behavioral or safety events; emergency medical events; allegations involving a student or staff member; or another significant incident reasonably requiring investigation.

When an incident report or other qualifying notification is generated, there should be a defined process for determining whether relevant video exists and preserving it. Access, review, retention, and disclosure should comply with FERPA and other applicable privacy requirements.

Section 9: Individual Student Needs and IEPs

Students have different needs and not every safeguard will apply identically to every student. Individual parents may separately request that their child's Case Conference Committee (CCC) determine whether electronic compliance tracking, daily parent reporting, video monitoring, or other safeguards should be incorporated into that child's IEP based upon individualized disability-related needs.

The existence or absence of a district-wide system should not prevent a CCC from considering an individual student's need for these supports. Students should not unnecessarily lose access to inclusion, nondisabled peers, appropriate programming, or educational opportunities simply because additional safeguards are required.

Formal Request to the District

  • Evaluate and implement a district-approved electronic care and support compliance system for appropriate high-support students.
  • Include active reminders, overdue alerts, escalation procedures, exception documentation, and an auditable record for identified critical supports.
  • Establish responsibility and backup-coverage procedures for critical student needs.
  • Provide participating families with an automatic daily electronic care and support report.
  • Establish supervisory review procedures capable of identifying repeated missed or delayed supports and other concerning patterns.
  • Implement district-operated video monitoring in appropriate high-support special education classrooms.
  • Establish procedures requiring preservation of potentially relevant video following significant incidents.
  • Identify what electronic tracking and video-monitoring capabilities currently exist within the district.
  • Develop appropriate procedures addressing privacy, data security, retention, authorized access, video review, and FERPA compliance.
  • Include affected families and special education staff in development of these procedures.
  • Establish a reasonable timeline for evaluation and implementation.
  • Provide affected families with a written response identifying the district's decisions, proposed actions, alternatives considered, and any legal, technological, financial, or policy barriers the district believes affect implementation.

Shared Objective

Students have different disabilities, abilities, educational programs, and individual needs. What many share is an extraordinary dependence upon adults combined with a limited ability to independently report when a need has been missed or explain what happened when something goes wrong. That combination creates a vulnerability that deserves safeguards designed specifically around it.

REQUIREMENT → REMINDER → COMPLETION → VERIFICATION → ALERT IF OVERDUE → ESCALATION → RESOLUTION → DAILY FAMILY REPORT

Video monitoring provides an additional objective safeguard when a significant event nevertheless occurs and the student cannot reliably explain what happened.

The objective is not surveillance or unnecessary paperwork. The objective is to prevent critical needs from being missed, verify that required supports are being provided, identify problems while they can still be corrected, keep families appropriately informed, protect students who cannot always advocate for themselves, and protect the educators entrusted with their care.

We ask RBBCSC to work collaboratively with affected families and educators to evaluate and establish appropriate safeguards.
